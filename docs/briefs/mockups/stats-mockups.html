Three ways to make the Statistics page nicer

Max, here are three propositions for Statistics, drawn as GNOME windows at desktop size and at 360 px. Like Wellbeing, they put each chart and its numbers in one rounded card, add soft grid lines, and get rid of the hatched grey. Use the switch to see them in dark mode.

The deck is your SIC 3101 deck. The numbers are invented so the charts have something to show.

  • Hatched grey for FailedIt looks like a rendering glitch, especially on a thin bar.
  • Charts float on the pageA dashed rule and a bare axis, with nothing holding them together.
  • Hard to read the values“Up to 50” is the only scale, and you have to select a bar to see any number.
  • Same weight for everythingThree charts of the same size, one after another.
Windows scroll inside, like the app. On a phone, swipe sideways to see the second window.

D · One week, softer Failed

Second round · in between A and B

B's layout: one card with a Reviews, Time and Forecast switch, today and this week at the top, seven bars with day letters, and arrows to go back a week. Failed is drawn the A way, in the same blue made lighter and set on top with a small gap, so there is no outline. There is no average line. Because colour alone is not enough for everyone, the screen-reader text and a tap on a day give the failed count. The three summary numbers stay a plain list underneath. Try the switch.

Desktop, 900 px

Phone, 360 px

From B

  • One week at a time, a short page.
  • The switch and the week arrows.
  • The summary as a list.

From A

  • Failed as a lighter blue with a gap, no outline and no hatching.
  • Grid lines with values and a dashed average line.

D · A month at a time

To compare with the week

The same card, but showing 30 days, with a date under each week. The arrows go back 30 days at a time. Today's bar has a light background behind it. Tap a day to see its numbers, as you can today. Forecast shows the next 30 days.

Desktop, 900 px

Phone, 360 px

Week

  • Wide bars with day letters, easy to read and tap, even on a phone.
  • A short span, so a single day stands out.

Month

  • Shows your habit over time, as today's page does.
  • Thin bars on a phone (about 7 px), so tapping one day is fiddly there.

A · Wellbeing cards

First round

The same three charts, each in a card of its own with its headline numbers at the top, as in Wellbeing: the day you selected (today to start with) and the 30-day total. Grid lines with values on the right replace “Up to 50”, and a dashed line marks your daily average. A failed review is the same blue made lighter, sitting on top of the passed part with a small gap.

Desktop, 900 px

Phone, 360 px

Good

  • Closest to GNOME's own look; calm.
  • Every chart says its key number without a click.
  • Keeps all of today's content, so nothing is lost.

Less good

  • Still a long page with three charts.
  • The lighter blue alone can be hard to see for some people, so the gap and the numbers in the header do the rest of the work.

B · One chart, three views

Most like Wellbeing

One card, exactly like Screen Time: a switch for Reviews, Time and Forecast, the numbers for today and this week, a week of bars with day letters, and arrows to go back a week. A failed review is an outlined segment, so it is shown by its shape and not only its colour. The three summary numbers become a plain list underneath. Try the switch in the mockup.

Desktop, 900 px

Phone, 360 px

Good

  • Short page, fits one screen, even on a phone.
  • Seven wide bars are easy to tap and read.

Less good

  • You see one week at a time instead of a month.
  • A switch and week arrows to build and to make work with the keyboard.

C · Calm summary

Fewest charts

Fewer bars, more plain words. A ring for expected retention, one bar split into Passed and Failed with each count written under it, a calendar of the last 12 weeks where darker squares mean more reviews, and the forecast as a list of days with small level bars.

Desktop, 900 px

Phone, 360 px

Good

  • Easy to read at a glance; the calendar shows your study habit nicely.
  • Failed is a labelled number, which works for everyone.

Less good

  • Time studied per day disappears (only the total stays).
  • The furthest from today's page, so the most work.

Chosen direction: D

After A, B and C, Max asked for something in between: B's calm, one-week layout, with A's lighter blue for Failed. That is D, at the top of this page.
