# 79. Deck page: grouped by tag, no boxes, a due word on each row

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-09
- Supersedes: the row look of ADR 0077 (the boxed list, the rows' tag pills on the deck page, All Cards' status on the second line)
- Refers to: Phase 3b brief, slice 3b; mockup `docs/briefs/mockups/deck-page-round2.html`, section `#e` (function `E()`), chosen by the maintainer after testing slice 3; ADR 0013 (the deck page's `GtkListView`), 0042 (card rows), 0044 (All Cards), 0065 (selection mode), 0073 (a row change costs no walk of the list), 0077 (shared tags, drawn math); GTK 4.24 sources cited below

## Context

With slice 3 built, the maintainer tried it on a deck of fronts only: the boxed list became a tall stack of identical grey bars, a heavy slab of card colour in a thin column, and a row without a back had nothing to say but its name. Of round 2's three propositions he chose E: keep "On every card:", drop the box, group the cards by the tags they have besides the shared ones so no row needs pills, and end each row with a short word for when the card is due. All Cards takes the flat rows and the word but not the groups: its filters do that job.

## Decision

**A flat list.** Both lists are a `GtkListView` with libadwaita's `separators` style class and no background of their own (`listview.card-list` keeps `background: none`), in a 640 px clamp; the rows are the same `CardRow`, laid out as action rows. The boxed-list CSS of ADR 0077 is gone. A row without a back has no second line (the deck page always hid it; ADR 0077's `min-height` still lines rows up when pills show).

**Groups are sections of a model of their own.** `widgets::CardGroups` is a `GListModel` and a `GtkSectionModel` over the deck page's `gio::ListStore`, which stays in creation order and is still changed item by item. Each group is a section; the list view gets a `header-factory` (GTK 4.12) making a `CardGroupHeader` per group on screen, and loses it while the cards are not grouped (`gtk_list_view_set_header_factory(NULL)` shows no headers). A `GtkSortListModel` with a section sorter was the other way: it would sort the cards (n log n per change) and know nothing of "too many groups". `GtkFlattenListModel` over one store per group would split the store every page action relies on.

- **The plan** (`card_groups::plan`, pure): the shared tags (`SharedTags`, as before), each card's extra tag ids hashed to its group in one walk, then a counting sort, so cards keep their order inside a group; only the groups are sorted (cards with no extra tag first, then by the name keys of their tags, tag by tag). Time is linear in the cards' tags. 10,000 cards with five tags each: 28 ms of CPU in a debug build; one change of a 10,000-card deck (walk, plan, diff): 19 ms.
- **When grouping helps** (`grouping_helps`, pure): two to six groups, and no more than half of them holding a single card. Otherwise the list shows plain rows in the store's order, each with its extra tags as pills and "+N", as slice 3 did. One group says nothing the shared tags do not.
- **Following the store.** The model connects to the store before anything else does, so every other handler of a change reads it up to date (handlers run in connection order). Each change plans again at once (deferring it, as `SharedTagsQueue` defers the shared tags, would leave the model disagreeing with the store in between) and passes on one `items-changed` for the span where the old and new orders differ (`changed_span`, by object): an edit that keeps a card's tags changes one row; a card that gains a tag changes the rows between its old and new place. When a group's start, size or tags changed it also emits `sections-changed` over the whole list, which makes the list view make its headers on screen again (`gtk_list_item_manager_model_sections_changed_cb`, gtk/gtklistitemmanager.c). The deck page's shared tags now come from the plan, once per change, instead of `SharedTagsQueue` (All Cards keeps the queue).
- **Two kinds of position.** The store's positions change the list; the model's are the rows'. The page reads the clicked row from the model (`shown_item`), focuses by the model's position (`shown_position`, `focus_card`), and after a delete or a move focuses the row now at the removed row's place in the list shown (`index_after_removal` on the model's positions). `ui::selection::Parts::items` is now the shown model (a `gio::ListModel`), so its Space, Ctrl+A, click and refocus work in the order the user sees; All Cards passes its store, which is what it shows.
- **Headers.** "Only the shared tags" over the cards without other tags, "Also" and the group's pills over the others, "14 cards" at the end (`card_groups::text`). With no shared tag at all (no "On every card:" line) the words are "No tags" and "Tagged" (all four with the gettext context "group header"), since "only the shared" and "also" would refer to nothing. A header is a `GtkListView` section header, so libadwaita pads it (18 px above, 6 px under); the text starts where the rows' does (14 px). It shows at most six pills and "+N", as the shared-tags line does (`card_list_header::show_capped`, now shared). Its role is Heading and its accessible label says it whole: "Also tagged Analyse, MAT3101, 7 cards". In selection mode the headers stay.

**The first header in sight.** ADR 0077 moved "On every card:" out of the list because focusing the first row before the list is laid out (every time the page opens) anchors that row at the top (`gtk_list_base_scroll_to_item` places an unallocated item at 0) and scrolls whatever is above it away. The keyboard does the same: Up, Page Up or Home onto the first row scrolls it just into view (`gtk_list_base_set_focus_child`). A group header has to be in the list, so the page undoes that scroll, and only that one: opening the page (`focus_when_shown`) and those keys (a capture-phase key controller on the list, `keep_first_header`) arm a check that runs on the next frames (`watch_first_header`: from the second frame, once the focus move's layout is done, for about a second on open and four frames after a key) and, if the first row has the focus and sits exactly at the top of a scrolled view, scrolls to the very top (`show_first_header`), which re-anchors the list there (`gtk_list_base_adjustment_value_changed_cb`) with the focus left on the row. A wheel, touchpad or scroll-bar scroll never arms it, so one that stops with the first row exactly at the top stays there. The decision is read from the row's place as laid out, with nothing remembered. Two earlier versions failed: one waited a fixed two frames on open and remembered the header's height from a scroll up at the top, and failed in CI when the layout took another number of frames, since the height was then never seen; the next checked after every scroll, so a slow scroll down from the top that stopped on the header's height was snapped back. Checked under Xvfb by `the_first_groups_header_stays_in_sight` (open, a scroll to the header's height left alone, Up from the second row), run 30 times with the test pinned to one CPU beside three busy loops, and on screenshots.

**The due word** (`card_row::text::due_word`, pure): "Today" (accent colour, bold, full strength) for a card that can be studied now (a review due or overdue, a learning step whose time has come); "In 10 min" or "In 5 h" for a step to come or a review later in this study day; "Tomorrow" in the next study day; "In N days" after, counted in study days from the next day's start; "New"; "Suspended" beside the pause icon. Overdue is "Today" too: the row says what to do. A screen reader hears "due today", "due in 3 days", appended to the row's label (`with_due`; a suspended card's label says "suspended" once). The rows follow two page properties, `now` and `next-day`: the deck page sets them when it opens, when it is shown, and when its study timer runs (the next card coming due, the next day); All Cards sets them when it reads its rows. A word changes on screen then, not every minute.

**Where a deck page's card stands.** `Store::deck_standings` (core, one query per deck) gives each card's `CardStatus` and whether it is new, replacing `new_card_ids` in the page; `refresh_standings` (was `refresh_new_flags`) replaces in one splice the items whose standing changed, as ADR 0073 asked of the flags. A single card's standing comes from `browse_row`.

**All Cards.** Flat rows, the due word at the end, its pills with "+N", and on the second line the deck, then the back ("Maths · …", the deck alone on a phone). The status left the second line, since the word says it; the row's accessible label still says the full status ("Due in 3 days").

Alternatives not taken:

- **Proposition D or F**: the maintainer chose E.
- **Grouping on All Cards**: its filters by deck, tag and state already narrow the list.
- **Groups shown however many there are**: past six, or with mostly single cards, the headers name one card's tags each; plain rows say it in less room.
- **A header above the scrolled list for the first group**, as ADR 0077 did for the shared tags: the groups would no longer be alike.
- **Ticking the due words every minute**: hundreds of rows redrawn for "In 9 min"; the page's own study timer already runs when a card comes due.

## Consequences

- A deck whose cards share tags and differ by a few shows them in groups with no pill on any row; the cards are no longer all in creation order on the page (they are inside each group, and the store and every action keep it).
- Deck pages and All Cards show when each card is due without opening it.
- Binding a row now says its due word: binding one row to 4,000 cards took about 460 ms of CPU in a debug build (380 ms before; the bound is 2 s).
- Each change of a deck page plans the groups again: linear in the deck's tags, about 19 ms for 10,000 cards in a debug build, once per change; a bulk action is one change (`reload`, `refresh_standings`).
- `fill_scratch_db --groups` adds the round 2 mockup's deck of fronts only (two groups, mixed due states) and "Tag sets" (a dozen tag sets: plain rows).
- Days in the due word are counted as 24 hours from the next study day's start: across a daylight-saving change, a card due within an hour of a day boundary can say "Tomorrow" for "In 2 days" or the reverse.
- After a delete or a move the focus goes to the row now at the removed row's place in the list shown; if that removal changes the groups (or ends them), that row is another card than the one under it before.
- With no tag shared, the French headers say "Étiquettes" and "Sans étiquette", and screen readers hear "Également : …" rather than an agreed participle.
- Not verified by eye: the maintainer's own session (fonts, scaling, the GL renderer), Orca reading the headers, and the snap to the top on a touchpad's kinetic scroll.
