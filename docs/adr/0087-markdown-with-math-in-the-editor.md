# 87. Markdown with math in the editor

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-16
- Refers to: ADR 0019 (Markdown), ADR 0021 (math rendering), ADR 0022 (the source-view editor); the 0.4.2 request "no Markdown styling inside math"

## Context

The card editor highlighted both sides with GtkSourceView's stock `markdown` language. It knows nothing of math, so in `$$n_1\sin\theta_1 = n_2\sin\theta_2$$` the text between the underscores went italic, and `*` or `**` inside a formula went italic or bold. The renderer (pulldown-cmark with math, ADR 0021) shows those as formulas, so the editor disagreed with the preview on the very cards that use math most.

## Decision

- Rappel ships its own language, `rappel-markdown` (`crates/app/data/language-specs/rappel-markdown.lang`), in the app's GResource. It copies nothing from `markdown.lang`: it refers to it (`markdown:markdown-syntax`, see below) and replaces six of its contexts with `<replace>`.
- **Math** is found as the renderer finds it: `$$…$$` is display math (over several lines, and here also ending at a blank line, so a `$$` typed alone does not colour the rest of the card); `$…$` opens on a `$` not followed by a space and closes on the next unescaped `$` not preceded by one, on the same line; a closing `$` followed by a digit is a price, not math (`$5-$10`, as `content::parse`'s `is_currency`); a backslash before ASCII punctuation escapes it, so `\$` is never a delimiter and `\\` is an escaped backslash (pulldown-cmark's `firstpass.rs`). Not modelled: braces hiding a `$` (`$\text{$}$`).
- Math has one quiet style, mapped to `def:inline-code` (a colour, no weight or slant): math is source the preview renders, like code. Nothing else styles it.
- **Emphasis, strong emphasis and `#` headings** look for their closing marker past whole math spans, and are styled piece by piece: their markers and the text between formulas, never the formula. A style on the whole span would make the math in it italic or bold too, since GtkSourceView's tags add up. Underscores no longer open or close emphasis inside a word (`x_1`), as in CommonMark.
- **Escapes and math** replace `markdown:backslash-escape`, which every Markdown context includes, so math is found in blockquotes too.
- **Loading.** GtkSourceView 5.4 and later read language files from a `resource:///` directory on a manager's search path (GtkSource-5.gir, `gtk_source_language_manager_set_search_path`). The search path can only change before a manager loads its first language, so the editor has a manager of its own (`card_editor::language`, one per thread, the main one), with the GResource directory before GtkSourceView's own. Nothing is installed beside the binary, so the snap and any other package need no change.
- **Long lines are left plain.** From 768 characters on, the rest of a line gets no highlighting at all (the `long-line` context, first among the root's, with the start `\G(?=.{768})`: `\G` is where a search resumes, the line's start or the end of a context of several lines that closes on it, so a line that starts inside display math closed at once is capped too (and, before HTML was dropped below, one inside an HTML tag); with `^` it was not, and `"$$\n$$"` or `"<a\n>"` followed by 40 KB of `$a$ ` took 31 s and 36 s in one step). GtkSourceView's engine spends time in proportion to a line's length for every piece it highlights on that line, and one line is never split across main-loop steps, so a line full of formulas costs the square of its length. Measured with GtkSourceView 5.22 before the cap: 20 KB of `$$a ` on one line took 2.4 s, `# ` and 10,000 `$$a ` 13.5 s, and a 1 MiB side would take hours, with the window frozen (the stock language has the same cost for a long line of code spans: 40 KB of `` `a` `` took 17 s). The cap is chosen by measure. GtkSourceView's idle worker highlights several lines per main-loop step, so what matters is a side of many lines just under the cap, full of formulas: 1 MiB of them gave longest steps of 1.0 to 1.2 s with a cap of 2,048 (the stock language on code spans behaves the same), 0.45 s with 1,024, 0.33 s with 768 and 0.20 s with 512. 768 keeps steps in the 0.2 to 0.4 s range while leaving most paragraphs highlighted. Measured in the editor's view under Xvfb (`hostile_one_line_sides_are_usable_in_the_text_field`, the bounds of ADR 0025 row 14, 2 s per step): a 1 MiB side on one line of `$$a `, `# $$a `, `# a$ `, `*a `, `_a `, `*$a `, or of `$a$ ` after `$$\n$$`, `<a\n>` or `<title>\n`, or of `a{} ` after `<style>\n`, or of `\a ` inside display math, gives longest steps of 0.17 to 0.22 s, most of it laying out the line, which the stock language costs too; lines of 764 characters full of `$a$ ` give 0.33 s. Highlighting such a side in full still takes a while in the background, in those steps.
- **HTML is no longer highlighted.** The language's root takes `markdown:markdown-syntax`, markdown's Markdown contexts, not `markdown:markdown`, which adds `html:html`. HTML in a card is never interpreted or rendered (SPEC §1.5): it shows as its text, so colouring tags in the editor suggested a meaning they do not have. And HTML's contexts of several lines (`<title>`, `<style>`, `<script>`, comments, a tag over two lines) never include the long-line cap, so their content was highlighted uncapped: measured with 5.22, `<title>` then 20 KB of `$a$ ` took 7.1 s in one step (the stock language 0.0 s, as it has no math), `<TITLE >` then `*a* ` 4.9 s (stock 1.5 s), and `<style>`, a tag over two lines or `<!--` then 1 MiB 7 to 14 s, in the stock language too. Without HTML each of these is plain text, and capped.
- **Two contexts of several lines are left: display math and markdown's ``` fence**, whose lines are not capped: inside display math only escapes are looked for, and the fence has no children, so neither creates anything costly. Measured: `$$` then 1 MiB of `\a ` on one line, 0.20 s per step in the editor (0.0 s to highlight 40 KB); ``` then 1 MiB of `$a$ `, 0.20 s.
- If the language cannot be loaded, the editor falls back to the stock `markdown` with a warning in the log, and to no highlighting without that.

## Consequences

Formulas look the same in the editor whatever they contain, and emphasis around a formula no longer swallows or breaks it. The tests load the language through its manager and check, character by character, which text is math and which is italic or bold, for the renderer's own cases (`content/tests.rs`). A paragraph of 768 characters or more on one line loses its highlighting from there on, and HTML tags are no longer coloured. The highlighting stays an approximation of the parser, as the stock one was: a line-based regex cannot see paragraphs, so display math can be closed by a later `$$` across a blank line in the renderer's view, and the brace rule above is not followed. A future GtkSourceView `markdown.lang` that renames the six replaced contexts would make the language fail to load: the fallback keeps the editor working, and the tests fail.
